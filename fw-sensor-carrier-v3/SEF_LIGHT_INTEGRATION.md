# SEF-light integration

## Milestones

1. Feed calibrated, timestamped IMU, barometer, GNSS, and magnetometer samples to SEF-light in time order. Verify full-rate delivery and bounded queues on the board.
2. Publish the selected SEF-light height, velocity, and attitude through one state watch. Verify the CAN vertical and orientation messages use that watch and the documented coordinate frames.
3. Tune from measured stationary noise and a known height change. Verify height convergence, sensor failover, and dynamic response on hardware.

The full-rate stationary sensor path is verified on hardware. The state watch supplies the CAN vertical and orientation payloads, but actual CAN frames have not been observed with a bus peer. Host tests cover a simulated lift and magnetic aiding; a known-height hardware move and magnetometer tumble calibration remain necessary to finish dynamic and heading verification.

The firmware's SEF-light and hysteretic selector dependencies are now included in this repository under `crates/asteria-sef-light` and `crates/asteria-estimator-selector`. Their source and tests came from the local `asteria-state-estimation` revision `bb5049e` and were formatted for this repository; the sibling checkout had no configured Git remote, so its former relative path was not reproducible from a fresh firmware checkout. The copied library's 25 tests, the firmware's 21 host tests, and the embedded release build pass from the in-repository paths.

A source-path audit found one spawned processing task, `state_estimation::task`. Its selected SEF-light state is the sole producer of `STATE_ESTIMATE_WATCH`; the CAN state task reads that watch for both orientation and vertical telemetry, and sends MSL height only after the selected IMU chain has a GNSS anchor. The `hermes-can` vertical-state message round-trip test passes with `message-vertical-state-data` enabled. This verifies construction and serialization, while the bus-peer check in milestone 2 remains open.

The full-run mean MSL heights were 420.98 m from GNSS_1 and 420.72 m from the selected estimator. Mean upward speeds were −0.002 m/s and +0.023 m/s respectively. The first-to-last ten-second height changes were +0.78 m for GNSS_1, +2.14 m for the estimator, and +5.09/+6.26 m for the raw pressure altitudes. The estimator remained close to the selected GNSS long-run mean, though a small positive velocity and short-run height drift remain. No physical lift test was possible in this run.

The ascent host test previously stamped a simulated NAV-PVT solution at its measurement epoch, whereas firmware stamps it when the packet is received. With a synthetic 99.6 ms delivery delay and 24 m/s upward speed, the corrected test's height was 2.16 m below truth. This is a timing sensitivity, not a measurement of this receiver's actual latency. The next board log records GNSS UTC epoch fields in a one-second status report. Comparing that report with host time includes report scheduling and clock offset, so it does not establish packet delivery latency; a known-height dynamic hardware run is still needed.

The same failure run revealed first-ten-second gyro peaks of 10.55 rad/s on IMU_0 and 12.33 rad/s on IMU_1 while the stationary accelerometers stayed near gravity. Later ten-second gyro peaks were below 0.01 rad/s. The [LSM6DSO32 application note](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/dm00696324-lsm6dso32-alwayson-3d-accelerometer-and-3d-gyroscope-stmicroelectronics.pdf) specifies gyro turn-on settling of about 70 ms plus three samples at 833 Hz. The readout now clears the FIFO, waits 75 ms after enabling the gyro, and starts FIFO collection afterward. In the first board run with this change, first-ten-second gyro peaks fell to 0.057 and 0.052 rad/s; both IMUs delivered roughly 800 samples/s, the estimator queue had zero drops or late samples, and GNSS_1 held 20.01 PVT/s with 50 ms maximum epoch gaps.

## Selection and output policy

The normal barometer-bias walk was raised from 0.001 to 0.02 m/√s after a stationary replay with steady 420 m GNSS and raw pressure altitudes drifting by 5.0 m and 4.0 m over five minutes. The old setting allowed 1.85 m maximum MSL error after the first minute; the new setting kept it below 1 m while the separate 7 m peak-to-peak indoor GNSS-wander replay still passed. This changes SEF-light's bias process uncertainty, not a barometer reference or a GNSS acceptance threshold. A 111 s stationary board run then accepted all 2,159 selected GNSS_1 height updates, with zero queue loss in eleven reports. First-to-last ten-second means moved −1.12 m for GNSS_1 and −1.41 m for the selected estimate; raw pressure altitude rose by 0.81 m and 2.51 m, while the fitted biases rose by 2.34 m and 4.21 m. The indoor GNSS movement prevents interpreting that difference as absolute height error.

GNSS_0 reported a 3D fix in 79 of that run's 107 one-second reports, so a short controlled trial suppressed GNSS_1 fusion from 50–65 s while continuing both UART readouts. GNSS_0 lost its 3D fix just before the interval and reported only 2D throughout it. No GNSS_0 height update was possible; the stationary estimate ranged from 420.23–421.24 m during the gap, and GNSS_1 updates resumed afterward. All seven queue reports had zero drops and late events. The temporary suppression was removed, the worktree returned to the committed source, and `just run --release` reflashed the production firmware; the next 17 s showed accepted GNSS_1 updates and both barometers and magnetometers streaming. A real valid-fix receiver handover remains unverified on hardware.

GNSS readout requires the UBX `GPS_FIX_OK` flag. Vertical fusion requires a finite 3D height and vertical velocity. Receiver `vAcc` and PDOP raise the observation uncertainty rather than disqualifying a valid fix. Among fresh receivers, lower computed height uncertainty wins; the selected receiver stays until the other is at least 1.5 times better. SEF-light receives only the chosen receiver, so it does not blend their heights. The 5σ innovation gate may still reject an extreme outlier. `vAcc` measures the height dimension directly, while PDOP supplies a floor when reported accuracy looks optimistic under poor geometry.

Each calibrated magnetometer supplies the attitude chain of the same index when its field magnitude is physically plausible. AHRS magnetic rejection handles angular disagreement, and samples expire after 250 ms. The selected IMU chain supplies both the published vertical state and orientation. The CAN orientation quaternion is the inverse of SEF-light's body-to-NED quaternion. Without magnetic calibration, roll and pitch remain available while yaw may drift.

Orientation is published once the selected IMU attitude is ready. Vertical CAN telemetry waits until the selected IMU chain has accepted a GNSS height update, including after an IMU handover, so a raw pressure altitude is never labeled MSL.

## Remaining verification

- Check the post-flash stationary run for new IMU selector behavior, temperature trends, and MSL anchoring after handover.
- Calibrate both magnetometers while tumbling the board, reset, then verify AHRS reports accepted magnetic samples. This requires physical movement.
- Measure a known vertical displacement and return to the start. Check MSL height and vertical-velocity response and recovery without changing the GNSS selection policy. An independent MSL reference is needed to establish absolute accuracy; the indoor stationary run cannot do that.
- Observe a real GNSS_1-to-GNSS_0 handover when both receivers have usable 3D fixes; the indoor backup receiver reported only 2D or no fix during the controlled outages.
- Observe actual CAN frames with a bus peer or analyzer. The firmware build verifies message construction, but no CAN bus was available for this run.
